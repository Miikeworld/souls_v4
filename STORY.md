The world discovered Corestone, a mineral capable of storing and releasing enormous amounts of magical energy.

At first it was treated as something supernatural. Over generations, people learned to refine it, contain it and build machines around it. Eventually, magic stopped being separate from technology.

Corestone powered furnaces, lifts, weapons, prosthetics, armour, factories and entire settlements.

That is why the world can naturally contain medieval ruins, Western armour and swords alongside mechanical machinery, powered suits, industrial foundries and magical technology.

The civilisation did not become futuristic. Instead, it developed along a completely different technological path built around Corestone engineering.

## Violet Core

When Corestone is properly refined and stabilised, it forms a Violet Core.

A Violet Core produces controlled purple energy.

Purple represents: stability, control, precision, technology and safe magical power.

The protagonist possesses an unusually powerful Violet Core integrated into their suit.

The suit distributes the Core's energy throughout the body through mechanical and magical channels.

This powers the player's: magic, suit abilities, energy attacks, enhanced movement and other supernatural abilities.

This is why the player's old Mana resource becomes **Core Energy**. Core Energy is simply the usable energy currently available from the protagonist's Violet Core. It is not traditional wizard mana.

## Crimson Core

Engineers eventually discovered they could force Corestone beyond its stable limits.

If enough energy is pushed into a Corestone system, the Violet Core can enter an unstable state and become a Crimson Core.

A Crimson Core produces far more raw energy than a Violet Core. But it is extremely difficult to control.

Red therefore represents: instability, destructive power, overheating, corruption and loss of control.

A Crimson Core is not inherently evil. It is essentially a Core being pushed beyond what it was designed to safely contain. That distinction is important.

The game's central visual relationship becomes:

- **Purple = power under control.**
- **Red = power exceeding control.**

## The Collapse

At the height of the old civilisation, researchers began producing Crimson Cores on an industrial scale.

The motivation wasn't simply greed. Stable Violet technology had limits. People wanted stronger machines, stronger weapons and more energy.

Eventually, one of the large-scale Crimson Core projects catastrophically failed. This event became known simply as the Collapse.

Corestone facilities overloaded across the region. Machines failed. Factories became unstable. Some Corestone deposits mutated. People exposed to large amounts of Crimson energy changed physically and mentally. Creatures living beneath the earth were also affected.

Civilisation broke apart.

Centuries later, the ruins remain. Some machines still operate. Some facilities are completely abandoned. Others continue following instructions given to them hundreds of years ago.

## The protagonist

The protagonist begins with incomplete memories.

They possess a specialised suit and a powerful Violet Core.

At first, they believe the Violet Core simply powers the suit. But this isn't completely true.

The suit was designed for two purposes: to channel the Core's energy... and to contain it.

The protagonist has an unusual connection to Corestone and can survive exposure that would normally seriously harm another person. Why they are capable of this becomes one of the game's main mysteries.

As the player unlocks new abilities, they are not randomly learning spells. They are gaining greater access to the Violet Core already inside the suit.

## Phase 2 — Shattered Warden

During the fight, his containment begins failing.

This is the form we've been designing with: dark Western armour, pale hair, broken silhouette, red cracks and floating fragments.

He gains: teleportation, Core magic, aggressive movement, magical projectiles and red energy attacks.

Importantly, this is not a traditional "Haha, now I will reveal my true power!" transformation.

The player is literally breaking the system that has kept him alive and contained the Crimson Core for years. His increasing strength is also killing him.

## Phase 3 — Forsaken form

Eventually the remaining containment fails. His armour breaks apart. His body becomes heavily saturated with Crimson energy. He becomes larger and physically more aggressive.

This is the final form we designed. He loses much of his precise fighting style and becomes far more violent.

## The Warden's greatsword

His Phase 3 weapon should not be the player's red greatsword. The difference should be obvious.

The player's weapon is: engineered, mechanical, intentionally constructed and controlled.

The Warden's weapon should look: ancient, corrupted, irregular and almost grown rather than manufactured.

Imagine a massive black slab of Corestone and ruined metal held together by red energy. It looks barely functional.

That visual contrast reinforces the story: the player is learning to control Crimson power. The Warden has been consumed by it.

## Final revelation

Near the end, the protagonist learns why their Violet Core is so unusual.

It is related to the original Corestone containment system involved in the Collapse. The suit was specifically built because an ordinary human couldn't safely interact with it.

That is why the player can use unusually powerful magic, Corestone machinery reacts to them and they can stabilise the Crimson greatsword.

The protagonist's entire journey has unknowingly been leading them back toward the origin of the Core.

## The central conflict

The story shouldn't end with "Red bad. Purple good." That would make everything we've built less interesting.

The real question is: **Can dangerous power be controlled responsibly, or will people always push it too far?**

The old civilisation believed control was absolute. It wasn't.

The Warden ultimately decided the only solution was containment forever.

The protagonist represents a third possibility: understanding both states of Corestone instead of fearing one and exploiting the other.

## Possible ending direction

I wouldn't lock the ending yet, but there are three obvious directions available.

- The protagonist could destroy the remaining Corestone system, preventing another Collapse but ending much of the world's Core-powered technology.
- They could restore and stabilise it, allowing Corestone technology to survive while taking the risk that humanity might eventually repeat its mistakes.
- Or there could eventually be a darker ending where the protagonist takes control of both Violet and Crimson power personally.

We don't need to decide that until the rest of the game exists.

## Terminology (canon for now)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Corestone | The magical mineral/energy material behind the world's technology |
| Violet Core | Stable Corestone power |
| Crimson Core | Unstable/overloaded Corestone power |
| Core Energy | The protagonist's usable Violet Core energy / replacement for Mana |
| Crimson Instability | How unstable the player's greatsword Core currently is |
| Overdrive | Temporarily unleashing the greatsword while the Violet Core stabilises it |
| The Collapse | Working name for the destruction of the old Corestone civilisation |
| The Last Warden | Working title for the final boss |

Everything else stays unnamed for now. Locations, worms, NPCs, weapons and factions earn proper names once we know more about their personalities and roles — that stops the world filling with fantasy-generator names before the game is even built.
